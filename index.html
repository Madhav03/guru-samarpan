About Sripada Srivallabha Charitamrutam

🕉️ The Divine Subject

Sripada Srivallabha is the first incarnation of Dattatreya in Kali Yuga. Born circa 1320 CE in Pithapuram (East Godavari, Andhra Pradesh), He performed countless miracles and disappeared at Kuruvapuram (Krishna River island) in 1350 CE at age 30.

✍️ The Author

Shankar Bhatt — a Brahmin pilgrim from Karnataka who personally received Sripada's grace and was commanded by Him to write this biography. The entire book is Shankar Bhatt's first-person pilgrimage narrative across all 51 chapters.

Chapter 1
The Divine Command at Kanyakumari
Shankar Bhatt begins his pilgrimage. At Kanyakumari, Goddess Kanyaka Parameswari appears and commands: "Go to Kuruvapuram for the darshan of Sripada Srivallabha." He proceeds to Marutva Malai where the entire hill reverberates with "Sripada Rajam Saranam Prapadye!" The tiger at the cave is revealed as the transformed Brahmin Vyaghreswara Sarma.
Chapter 2
Siddhendra Yogi and Siddha Yogindra
In the Kadamba forest, Siddhendra Yogi reveals the sacred history of Vasavi Kanyaka Parameswari and the Indra-consecrated Shiva linga. Near Madurai, a divine three-hooded serpent with diamond-studded hoods guides Shankar Bhatt to Siddha Yogindra's ashram.
Chs. 3–4
Palaniswami — The 300-Year-Old Snake Siddha
In the hills near Chidambaram, Shankar Bhatt meets Palaniswami — a 300-year-old hermit who lives by inhabiting the corpse of a dead youth. Palaniswami revives the snake-bitten Madhava Namboodri using a live cobra as antidote, and reveals the four types of snakes and their cosmic significance.
Chs. 3–4 cont.
Arrival at Kuruvapuram — Sripada's Footprints
PRIMARY DESTINATION. Shankar Bhatt reaches the sacred Krishna River island of Kuruvapuram. He receives darshan of Sripada's divine footprints and the vision of Vasavambika. Here the greatest cosmic revelations unfold: Jesus-Dhruva connection, Muhammad-Allah, Adama-Havyavati, and the vision of Sai Baba.
Chapter 5
Tirupati, Kanipakam & the Karmic Gatekeeper
Shankar Bhatt visits Tirupati (Venkateswara = Sripada in his view) then reaches Kanipakam — the seat of the svayambhu Sri Varasiddhi Vinayaka. Here he meets Tirumala Das, a 70-year-old washerman-devotee at the temple. Tirumala Das reveals he was a learned Vedic scholar in his previous birth who was reborn as a washerman due to his miserly final thoughts at death. He now receives temple prasadam through trained dogs — a humble life transformed by devotion. PIVOTAL MOMENT: Tirumala Das commands Shankar Bhatt to begin writing the Charitamrutam and directs him to seek Vatsavayi Narasimha Varma at Pithapuram. He also narrates the miraculous origin of the Kanipakam idol: three quarrelling brothers healed when a svayambhu Vinayaka emerged from a well they were digging. Without this meeting, the entire Charitamrutam biography — and all the soul-prophecies it contains — might never have been recorded.
Chs. 6–7
Pithapuram — Sripada's Birthplace and Family
Shankar Bhatt arrives at Pithapuram, the Maha Samsthanam. He learns of Sripada's family: grandfather Bapannavadhani (reincarnation of Nanda), grandmother Rajamamba, father Appalaraja Sarma (reincarnation of Vishnu Datta), mother Sumati Maharani (reincarnation of Somidevamma).
Chs. 8–10
Vyaghreswara Sarma — The Tiger Liberation
Sripada directed the dunce Brahmin Vyaghreswara Sarma to Badrinath in a dream. He meditated on tigers, became a tiger, and Sripada walked on water to meet him at the Krishna River. Sripada rode him across the river; the tiger's skin became Sripada's seat. Final liberation attained.
Chs. 9, 11, 41
Siblings — Healed and Prophesied
Srīdhararaja Sarma (born blind, healed; next life: Samardha Ramadas, guru of Shivaji) and Ramaraja Sarma (born lame, healed; next life: Avadhuta Sridhara / Sridhar Swami). Both are partial manifestations of Dattatreya. Vasavi Kanyaka — the cosmic sister who gave power enabling Mary's virgin birth.
Chs. 12–15
Vatsavayi Narasimha Varma — Future Shivaji
Narasimha Varma, the Kshatriya nobleman and Sripada's closest devotee, is revealed to reincarnate as Shivaji Maharaja. Sripada affectionately called him "grandfather." He was present for the divine marriage proclamation of Sripada's parents.
Chs. 13–16
Pynda Venkatappayya Sreshti — The Wealthy Devotee
The wealthy Vaishya merchant devotee of Pithapuram. Sripada gifted a house to the poor Appalaraja Sarma at his devotion. Sripada once teased him: "Grandfather, do we belong to the Markandeya lineage?" — hinting at their deep soul connection. Runs throughout the book.
Chapter 15
The Arrogant Priest and the Maharaja
The priest of the Kukkuteswara Shiva temple and the local Maharaja refused to follow Sripada's commands. The Maharaja lost all his powers by the Lord's grace. The priest's future births were also prophesied.
Chs. 17–19
Dhandiswami — The Mute Monk
Dhandiswami, an arrogant monk who challenged Sripada's divinity, was struck mute as a lesson in humility. The episode illustrates the fatal danger of approaching a divine avatar with ego and scepticism.
Chs. 20–21
Raghavendra Swami — Prophesied Centuries Earlier
Sripada prophesied the coming of Raghavendra Swami (1595–1671 CE) — an incarnation of Prahlada — centuries before his birth. The shrine at Mantralayam on the Tungabhadra river was thus cosmically ordained by Sripada Himself.
Chs. 22–24
Vallabha Das — The Cobbler-Healer
Vallabha Das, the cobbler devotee of Sripada, was given the power to heal all diseases. He became a renowned healer in Pithapuram, embodying Sripada's teaching that the Lord's grace transcends all caste distinctions.
Chs. 25–26
Abbanna the Snake Charmer
Abbanna, the snake charmer, was given a sacred task by Sripada. The cosmic relationship between serpents and divinity — a recurring theme — is explored through Abbanna's devotion and service.
Chs. 27–30
Vasavi Kanyaka — World Mother and Cosmic Sister
Vasavi Kanyaka Parameswari, identified as Sripada's cosmic sister, is an incarnation of the World Mother. On Sravana full moon she ties raksha bandhan to Sripada. Her divine power was given to the soul of Mary enabling Jesus Christ's virgin birth. The story of Brihat Sila Nagaram (now Penugonda) is revealed.
Chs. 31–32
The Interfaith Cosmic Revelation
THE MOST REMARKABLE SECTION. Sripada reveals: (1) Jesus Christ = Dhruva reborn in Kali Yuga; called Narayana "Father." (2) Muhammad (Maha Mathi) = devotee of formless Makkeshwar (Allah). (3) Adama and Havyavati (Adam and Eve) in the "western island" — Kali entered as a serpent. All divine names flow from one Parabrahman.
Chs. 33–35
Vedanta Sarma — The Soul-Splitting
The extraordinary episode where Vedanta Sarma's soul was simultaneously incarnated as himself, his three wives, cobbler Bangarayya, AND a cow. Next life: priests at Kuruvapuram. Illustrates Sripada's omnipotence over the cycles of karma and rebirth.
Chs. 36–37
Dharma Gupta — The Long-Distance Devotee
Dharma Gupta became Shankar Bhatt's long-term travel companion after meeting him in the Pithapuram area. His story of encountering Sripada's grace and his steadfast devotion over long years of wandering is narrated at length.
Chs. 38–39
Bangārappa — Black Magic Reversed
Bangārappa attempted to use black magic against a devotee. The magic fell back on him with full force, resulting in his suffering. A powerful warning about the impossibility of harming those under Sripada's protection.
Chs. 40–46
Tripurantakam — Bhaskara Sastry's Revelation
THE CRUCIAL SECTION. Bhaskara Sastry, priest at Tripurantakam, gives Shankar Bhatt the fullest account of why Sripada left Pithapuram. He receives Sripada's copper sandals — a supreme blessing. All events after Sripada's disappearance from public view are narrated here.
Chs. 46–48
Journey to Kashi and the North
Shankar Bhatt journeys to Kashi/Varanasi with the old sanyasi whom Sripada had directed there. Sripada had told him: "Go to Kashi, live there, leave your body there." The eternal city is described in its cosmic significance.
Chs. 49–50
The Himalayas — Badrinath and Kedarnath
The pilgrimage reaches the Himalayas. Badrinath (where Vyaghreswara Sarma had earlier meditated, meeting Babaji's lineage) and Kedarnath — Lord Shiva's Jyotirlinga. The cosmic journey reaches its northern culmination.
Chapter 51
Conclusion — The Eternal Presence
Shankar Bhatt concludes the narrative. Sripada declares His eternal presence: "I am always at Kuruvapuram. Those who come with faith will never leave empty-handed." The prophecy of Narasimha Saraswati and Swami Samarth as future avatars is confirmed. Sripada Rajam Saranam Prapadye.

🌟 Core Teaching: Universal Oneness
The cosmic theology of the Charitamrutam
The Charitamrutam makes a radical declaration: all divine names across all traditions point to one Parabrahman. • Makkeshwar (Lord of Mecca) = the same who is called Allah — formless, infinite • Jesus = Dhruva, reborn in a Western island; Mary received Vasavi Kanyaka's power • Adama and Havyavati (Adam and Eve) are in the Bhavishya Purana • Sripada personally visited Hanuman in Nepal and asked him to incarnate as Sai Baba and "worship God as Allah" Teaching: There is no barrier between traditions in the vision of a true Guru.
📿 The Three Dattatreya Avatars
As prophesied by Sripada Srivallabha Himself
Sripada declared the three Kali Yuga avataras of Dattatreya: 1. Sripada Srivallabha — born Pithapuram (1320s CE), disappeared Kuruvapuram (1350 CE) 2. Narasimha Saraswati — born Lad Karanja (circa 1378 CE), seat at Ganagapura, departed Kardali Vana (1459 CE) 3. Swami Samarth — seat at Akkalkot (~1800–1878 CE) Sripada also indicated that Shirdi Sai Baba would carry the divine light forward — the fourth in this cosmic chain. The Charitamrutam itself contains a vision of Sai Baba at Kuruvapuram — centuries before Sai Baba was born.

⚡ The Guru Tattva Teaching
On the nature of the Sadguru
"The Guru is not a person — He is a principle (tattva). The Sadguru is the living embodiment of Parabrahman. His grace descends regardless of time and space — a sincere devotee separated by centuries can still receive His direct blessing." The Charitamrutam itself is considered a living Guru — reading it with devotion and surrender is equivalent to receiving direct initiation from Sripada. His grace is not bound by His physical presence at Kuruvapuram in 1350 CE.
🏛️ Historical Context — 14th Century Deccan
The world in which Sripada appeared
Sripada's era (circa 1320–1350 CE): • The Vijayanagara Empire is newly founded (1336 CE) — the great Hindu bastion of South India • The Delhi Sultanate controls North India under the Tughlaq dynasty • The Bahmani Sultanate is rising in the Deccan • Kabir (1440–1518) will come later; Mirabai (1498–1547) even later • Namdev (1270–1350) is Sripada's approximate contemporary Sripada's cosmic revelations — unifying Hindu, Islamic, and even Abrahamic traditions — were centuries ahead of their time. In an era of religious conflict, He declared the unity of all divine names.

Parable of the Five Sheaths — What You Are Not
ParableChapter 18 — Vedantic TeachingJnana Teaching
Sripada explains the Pancha Kosha (five sheaths) through the parable of a lamp inside five nested boxes: the innermost box holds the lamp (the Self/Atman); around it are four increasingly opaque boxes — bliss body, wisdom body, mind body, prana body, and outermost the food body (the physical form). Most people believe they ARE the outermost box. The spiritual journey is simply opening box after box, recognising "not this, not this" (Neti, Neti), until the lamp is reached. "You cannot become the lamp," Sripada told Shankar Bhatt. "You already are. You just need to open the boxes."

Parable of the Pot and the Sky
ParableChapter 20Advaita Teaching
Sripada holds up a clay pot and asks Shankar Bhatt: "The sky inside this pot — is it different from the sky outside?" Shankar Bhatt sees immediately: the pot's clay creates the apparent boundary, but the sky-space inside and outside is the same continuous sky. "Break the pot," says Sripada. "Where did the inside sky go?" It merged with the outside. "This is birth, life, and moksha," Sripada explains. "The clay (body) creates the apparent boundary. The Atman inside is the same as Brahman outside. Death breaks the pot. Liberation is knowing — before death — that the sky was never separate." The Charitamrutam presents this as one of Sripada's core philosophical parables.

Siddhendra Yogi functions as the Charitamrutam's first great teacher — preparing Shankar Bhatt's consciousness to receive what Sripada Himself will later reveal.

Adam and Eve — Adama and Havyavati of the Bhavishya Purana
Cosmic RevelationChapter 29–31Biblical Connection
The Charitamrutam makes the extraordinary identification: the biblical Adam and Eve are "Adama and Havyavati" mentioned in the Bhavishya Purana — the Purana of prophecies. The garden of Eden corresponds to a cosmic garden described in Vedic cosmology. Their "fall" corresponds to the descent of consciousness into duality — the same as the Hindu concept of Maya's veil descending. The Charitamrutam presents this not as Hindu appropriation of Christianity but as the same event described in two different civilisational languages — both true, both incomplete without each other.

The Atmalinga of Gokarna — Ravana's Sacred Error
Cosmic EpisodeReferenced in Chapter 28Ramayana Connection
The Charitamrutam references the story of the Atmalinga — the Self-Linga of Shiva which Ravana obtained from Kailash through extraordinary tapas. Shiva gave it on condition: Ravana must not set it down before reaching Lanka. Ganesha (disguised as a boy) tricked Ravana into setting it down at Gokarna — where it became permanently fixed. The Charitamrutam uses this episode to explain why Gokarna is one of the most powerful Shiva shrines: it holds the Atmalinga intended for Lanka — the most powerful Linga Shiva ever gave to a mortal. Sripada visited Gokarna during His pilgrimage. The cosmic geography of the Charitamrutam connects Pithapuram, Gokarna, and Kuruvapuram as three points of a divine triangle.

Sripada Revives His Own Father
MiracleChapter 8Family Episode
When Sripada's father Appalaraja Sarma died of sudden illness, the household was plunged into grief. The young Sripada — only a child — walked calmly to his father's body, placed both hands on the chest, and sat in silence for a few minutes. His father's breathing resumed. He sat up. When asked by family members how this happened, Sripada said only: "Father wasn't done yet. He had more dharma to fulfil." The episode establishes that Sripada's miraculous power was not something He acquired through tapas or initiation — it was inherent from birth. He was born fully realised.

The Vatsavayi Chieftain's Devotion
Historical EpisodeChapter 6–7Royal Devotion
The ruling Vatsavayi family of Pithapuram were among the earliest and most fervent devotees of Sripada. The chieftain Narasimha Varma himself received Sripada's direct blessing — and his subsequent prosperity was attributed to Sripada's grace. The Charitamrutam records several episodes where the chieftain's court witnessed miracles: Sripada appearing in two places simultaneously, knowing events happening in distant villages, and predicting the outcomes of the chieftain's administrative decisions with perfect accuracy. The royal patronage gave Sripada's presence at Pithapuram official legitimacy — the king himself bowed.

The Descent of the Godavari — Dakshina Ganga
River OriginChapter 2 & 48Sacred Geography
The Charitamrutam explains the Godavari's celestial origin: Brahma requested Vishnu that the purifying energy of the Ganga flow through the Deccan for the benefit of souls who could not reach the northern Ganga. Vishnu sent this energy southward; it emerged as the Godavari at Brahmagiri (Tryambakeshvara), where Shiva's Jyotirlinga marks the spot. Called "Dakshina Ganga" (the Southern Ganga), the Godavari carries the same liberation-granting power. Shankar Bhatt's pilgrimage begins near this sacred arc — the entire narrative of the Charitamrutam unfolds within the sacred zone between the Godavari (north) and the Krishna (south).

The Sacred Origin of the Krishna (Krishnaveni)
River OriginMultiple ChaptersSacred Geography
The Charitamrutam refers to the Krishna river as "Krishnaveni" — containing the essence of Lord Krishna Himself. The river rises at Mahabaleshwar in the Western Ghats and flows east through Karnataka and Andhra. The Charitamrutam explains that the Krishna river was consecrated when Lord Krishna's divine energy was infused into it at the moment of His cosmic dissolution — making it one of the most spiritually potent rivers of the Deccan. Sripada's chosen seat at Kuruvapuram — a river island — is not accidental: the Krishna itself is His throne.

The Tungabhadra — River of the Southern Ramayana

The Tungabhadra — which joins the Krishna — is the river at whose banks Kishkindha (the monkey kingdom of the Ramayana) stood. Hanuman's homeland. The Charitamrutam's revelation that Sripada personally visited Hanuman in Nepal links the two rivers cosmically: Hanuman's Kishkindha sat on the Tungabhadra; Sripada's Kuruvapuram sits on the Krishna. When these two rivers merge, so do the two divine streams — Ramayana and the Datta Parampara — flowing into one sacred geography.

Vasavi Kanyaka's Fire Sacrifice — The Vow That Shaped Pithapuram

Centuries before Sripada's birth, the merchant community of Pithapuram was oppressed by Virabhadra, a Kshatriya chieftain who demanded the most beautiful girl of the community — Vasavi Kanyaka — as his wife. Vasavi Kanyaka refused: she had vowed celibacy and consecrated her life to God. When the chieftain threatened to destroy the entire community if she refused, Vasavi Kanyaka made a cosmic vow: "If I cannot live in honour, I will not live." She led 16 young virgins of her community in a fire sacrifice — entering the flames rather than submitting to dishonour. The Charitamrutam reveals that this act of supreme dharmic sacrifice created a permanent spiritual vortex at Pithapuram. It was specifically this accumulated sacrificial energy that made Pithapuram the right birthplace for Dattatreya's first modern incarnation. Sripada is born into the fire Vasavi created.

Sumathi's Prayer — The Mother Who Asked for God as Her Son
Divine Birth StoryChapter 1 — Before the BirthMother's Piety
Sumathi Maharani (Sripada's mother) was no ordinary devotee. The Charitamrutam records that she performed intense tapas for years, praying not for wealth, health, or children in the ordinary sense — but that the Lord Himself would take birth through her. Her prayer was precise and extraordinary: "I want You as my son." The cosmos responded. At the moment of Sripada's birth, the house filled with fragrance, music was heard without musicians, and the newborn sat in padmasana before the astonished midwife. Sumathi knew instantly: her son was not her son. He had arrived on loan. This knowing shaped her entire relationship with Sripada — she served Him as a devotee, not only as a mother.

Sripada Takes Sannyasa — Against All Family Wishes
RenunciationChapter 9The Great Departure
At a young age — against the vigorous protests of His family, who had already arranged His marriage — Sripada took sannyasa (formal renunciation). He did not ask permission. He did not negotiate. He calmly placed the sacred thread down, spoke His truth to His parents, and walked out. His mother wept. His father argued. But Sripada had already seen through the illusion of family bonds as an ordinary son — He was Dattatreya. He could not play the role for long. The Charitamrutam presents this departure not as cruelty but as the highest gift: His presence in the world as a free avadhuta would eventually liberate hundreds of thousands. Keeping Him in a household would have served one family. Freeing Him served all.

Sripada and the Nath Tradition — The Hidden Thread

The Charitamrutam reveals that the Nath Sampradaya — the tradition of Macchindranath, Gorakshanath, and the great siddhas of medieval India — is not separate from the Datta Parampara. It is the same river seen from the northern bank. Dattatreya initiated both streams. When Shankar Bhatt asks why the Nath tradition doesn't mention Dattatreya explicitly, Sripada smiles and says: "The river doesn't mention the mountain it descended from. It simply flows." The great Nath siddhas were Datta devotees in essence even when they used different names and forms. The Charitamrutam locates Sripada at the junction of these streams — not as founder of one but as the source of all.

The 72 Crore Yojana Teaching — Vedic Cosmology Explained

In one of the Charitamrutam's most remarkable episodes, Sripada explains the Vedic model of the cosmos to Shankar Bhatt — not as mythology but as precise spiritual geography. He describes the 14 lokas (worlds), the measurement system in yojanas, the placement of the Saptarishis (seven great sages) in the stellar realm, and how the liberated souls travel through the cosmic grid. What astonishes Shankar Bhatt is not the content — it is that Sripada describes each realm as somewhere He has been, as a traveller describes cities He has visited. He is not reciting scripture. The Charitamrutam records that Meenakshi never asked for a single miracle or blessing. She just cooked and served. One day Sripada called her into His presence and said: "You think you are serving me. But look." He opened her awareness: she saw her entire family's accumulated karma dissolving in the act of cooking for the Lord. Her husband's chronic illness lifted that week. Her son's failing prospects reversed within a month. She had been receiving miracles continuously — she just hadn't noticed, because she wasn't looking for them.

The great pontiff said simply: "Because He is what all the Upanishads describe. I have been teaching that reality all my life. Today I saw it." He became a devotee. Parable of the Seed and the Fruit — On Karma's Timing
ParableChapter 26Karma Teaching
A devotee asks Sripada: "Why do bad people prosper and good people suffer? Where is the justice?" Sripada points to a mango tree in fruit: "When was this seed planted?" The devotee calculates — perhaps twenty years ago. "So the man who planted the seed is not necessarily the man eating the fruit," Sripada says. "And the man eating the fruit may not remember planting the seed." Karma's accounting spans lifetimes, not years. The good man suffering today planted differently in another life. The bad man prospering today will account for this life's seeds — in another season, in another body. "But," Sripada adds, "the man who comes to the Guru skips the queue. The Guru burns the seeds before they can fruit."

Parable of the River and the Ocean — On Moksha
ParableChapter 44Liberation Teaching
A scholar asks Sripada: "When I attain moksha, do I lose myself? I am afraid of dissolution." Sripada asks: "Is the river afraid to reach the ocean?" The scholar thinks. "It loses its banks," he says. "It loses its name," Sripada agrees. "But does it lose its water?" No. The water remains — it simply becomes the ocean. "You will not lose your awareness in moksha," Sripada explains. "You will gain every other awareness. You were a cup of ocean worrying about losing yourself. The ocean doesn't miss the cup. It celebrates the water returning home." The scholar wept with relief.
About Shri Gurucharitra

🕉️ The Divine Subject

Narasimha Saraswati — the second Dattatreya avatar in Kali Yuga. Born circa 1378 CE in Lad Karanja (Karanjnagari), Maharashtra. His primary seat was Ganagapura (confluence of Bhima and Amaraja rivers, Karnataka). He departed from Kardali Vana near Srisailam in 1459 CE.

✍️ The Author

Saraswati Gangadhara — a direct disciple of Narasimha Saraswati. The book is composed in Marathi Ovibaddha (verse form) as a dialogue between Siddhamuni (the author's disciple) and Namdharak (a devoted seeker). Written c.15th–16th century CE.

📚 The Book

  • 52 chapters (Adhyayas)
  • Marathi language (Ovibaddha verse)
  • Covers both Sripada Srivallabha AND Narasimha Saraswati
  • One of the most sacred texts of the Datta tradition
  • Reading the entire Gurucharitra is considered a 7-day parayan

🔱 Prophesied by Sripada

In the Charitamrutam, Sripada Srivallabha directly prophesied the coming of Narasimha Saraswati as the second Dattatreya avatar. Sripada's ashes spiritually fell at Ganagapura, consecrating it centuries before Narasimha Saraswati made it His seat.

🛕 Primary Sacred Sites

  • Ganagapura / Gangapur — main 20-year seat (Karnataka)
  • Narsoba Wadi (Narsobachi Wadi) — confluence of Krishna & Panchaganga (Maharashtra)
  • Audumbar — sacred fig tree grove on the Krishna
  • Kardali Vana — final disappearance near Srisailam
  • Kashi — where He took sanyas diksha

📿 Significance

The Gurucharitra is not merely a biography — it is a comprehensive manual of devotional life (dharma, parayan, fasting, service to Guru) whose regular reading is said to remove all afflictions. Reading it on certain tithis (lunar days) grants specific blessings according to tradition.

Key Teachings — Shri Gurucharitra
🕉️ Guru Brahma Shloka
The foundational verse on Guru Tattva — from Guru Gita
Guru Brahma Guru Vishnu
Guru Devo Maheshwaraha
Guru Saakshaat Parabrahma
Tasmai Shri Guruve Namaha
Meaning: "The Guru is Brahma (Creator), the Guru is Vishnu (Sustainer), the Guru is Maheshwara (Destroyer). The Guru is verily the supreme Parabrahman. Salutations to that Guru." This shloka is the foundational teaching of the Gurucharitra — that the human Guru is not separate from God but is God's direct expression in this world. Narasimha Saraswati embodied this truth completely. The Gurucharitra is built as a demonstration of this teaching through hundreds of miraculous episodes.
🔔 Mangalacharana — Opening Mantra
Opening invocation of the Gurucharitra
Digambara Digambara
Shripad Vallabh Digambara
Digambara Digambara
Shripad Vallabh Digambara
"Digambara" means "sky-clad / wearing the directions" — the avadhuta who is free of all bodily coverings. This mantra acknowledges Sripada Srivallabha as the first avatar, whose blessings the author invokes before narrating the Gurucharitra. The Gurucharitra sees itself as a continuation of Sripada's cosmic work through Narasimha Saraswati — thus begins by honouring the first avatar.
📖 Parayan Vidhi — Sacred Reading Practice
Traditional guidelines for the Gurucharitra parayan
The Gurucharitra prescribes a specific parayan (sacred reading) practice: • Saptaah (7-day reading): One complete reading in 7 days • Trivaara (3-day reading): Chapters 1–24 on Day 1, 25–40 on Day 2, 41–52 on Day 3 • Nityaparayan (daily reading): One chapter per day The text teaches that sincere parayan with faith removes all sins, fulfills desires, cures diseases, and ultimately grants liberation. It is considered a living Guru — not merely a book. The Gurucharitra itself says: "One who reads this with faith obtains all that he desires and ultimately merges with the Supreme."
🌊 The 24 Gurus of Dattatreya
As narrated in the Gurucharitra — supreme wisdom of the avadhuta
Dattatreya taught that every element of nature was His guru: Earth · Water · Fire · Air · Sky · Moon · Sun · Pigeon · Python · Ocean · Moth · Bee · Elephant · Honey-gatherer · Deer · Fish · Courtesan Pingala · Osprey · Arrow-maker · Child · Moon · Snake · Spider · Wasp. Each taught a specific spiritual truth. This wisdom — that all of creation is the Guru's teaching — is the essential Dattatreya philosophy underlying the Gurucharitra. The avadhuta is one who has learned from all 24 and is thus free of fixed views.
✨ Shraddha Teaching
Central theme of the Gurucharitra
"Shraddha is the supreme virtue. Without it, all religious acts are meaningless. With it, even a simple act of service to the Guru bears infinite fruit." The Gurucharitra is filled with miraculous stories of devotees whose unwavering faith moved Narasimha Saraswati to perform miracles — restoring dead bodies to life, curing diseases, multiplying food, defeating rival scholars, blessing devotees with children. All this to teach that the Guru's grace is limitless when met with genuine surrender. This same teaching appears as "Shraddha and Saburi" in Book 3 (Sai Satcharitra).
🏛️ Historical Context — 15th Century Deccan
The world in which Narasimha Saraswati appeared
Narasimha Saraswati's era (circa 1378–1459 CE): • The Vijayanagara Empire is at its peak — Devaraya II (1424–1446) rules • The Bahmani Sultanate controls the Deccan plateau • This is the golden age of Marathi Bhakti — Sant Eknath (1533–1599) will later write the most authoritative commentary on the Gurucharitra • Kabir (1440–1518) is a younger contemporary; the Bhakti movement is in full flower • The Delhi Sultanate is weakening; the Lodhi dynasty takes power (1451) The Datta tradition under Narasimha Saraswati bridged the Shaiva-Vaishnava traditions and the emerging Bhakti movement.

About Sai Satcharitra

🕉️ The Divine Subject

Shirdi Sai Baba — a divine master who lived at Shirdi (Ahmednagar district, Maharashtra) for approximately 60 years. Birth date unknown (circa 1835–1838 CE). Mahanirvana (Samadhi): October 15, 1918. Transcended all religious boundaries — equally revered by Hindus and Muslims.

✍️ The Author

Govind Raghunath Dabholkar, pen name Hemadpant — a retired Deputy Collector who became a devoted disciple of Sai Baba. He began writing on Sai Baba's direct command. The Satcharitra was completed and published in 1922, four years after Sai Baba's mahanirvana.

📚 The Book

  • 51 chapters
  • Marathi language (later translated to all major Indian languages and English)
  • Written as Hemadpant's first-person account combined with stories of other devotees
  • Contains Sai Baba's own direct teachings, conversations, and parables

🔱 Connection to Datta Parampara

In the Sripada Charitamrutam, Sripada Srivallabha directly prophesied Sai Baba's coming, conferring His power on him. Sripada personally visited Hanuman in Nepal and asked him to incarnate and worship Him in the form of Allah. The chain: Sripada → Babaji → Lahiri Mahasaya → Sai Baba.

🛕 Shirdi — The Sacred Village

  • Dwarkamai — the mosque-temple where Sai lived 60 years
  • Chavadi — the building where He slept on alternate nights
  • Lendi Baug — the garden He tended daily
  • Gurusthan — the neem tree where He first appeared as a youth
  • Samadhi Mandir — His mahanirvana site and permanent abode

📿 Central Teachings

  • Shraddha (Faith) + Saburi (Patience) — Sai's two primary commandments
  • "Allah Malik" — God is the master; He is one
  • All religions lead to one God — He read Quran and Ramayan equally
  • "Why do you fear when I am here?"
  • Giving dakshina (coins) as a teaching tool — returning ego to God

Sabka Malik Ek — Allah Malik
Sai Baba's Universal Declaration
Sabka Malik Ek
Everyone's Master is One
Sai Baba's most repeated phrase — spoken in Urdu. He used both "Allah Malik" (God is the Master) and "Ram Ram" (the Hindu name) with equal reverence. This teaching dissolved the Hindu-Muslim divide in 19th-century Maharashtra. The Dwarkamai was open to all regardless of faith, caste, or background.
Shraddha and Saburi
The Two Pillars of Sai Baba's Teaching
Shraddha · Saburi
Faith · Patient Endurance
Sai Baba summarised His entire teaching in two words. Shraddha (श्रद्धा) — total faith in the Guru and in God. Saburi (सबुरी) — patient waiting, endurance, persistence without anxiety. He said: "These two are the ships that will carry you across the ocean of existence. Faith without patience is incomplete; patience without faith is empty."
Why Fear When I Am Here?
Sai Baba's Promise of Constant Protection
Why fear when I am here?
Sai Baba's Eternal Assurance
Spoken to devotees in distress, Sai Baba gave this absolute assurance repeatedly. He also said: "Look to Me and I shall look to you." These phrases are not metaphors but literal promises — documented instances show Sai Baba appearing in dreams and visions to protect devotees thousands of miles away from Shirdi at the exact moment of their crisis.
The Eleven Promises
From Sai Satcharitra — Phala Shruti (Chapter XLIX–L)
Sai Baba's eleven promises to those who read the Satcharitra with faith: 1. I shall remove the troubles of my devotees 2. I shall protect my devotees in times of calamity 3. Those who come to me will not want for food or clothing 4. The wicked-minded will not succeed in their aim 5. The devoted will get their heart's desire 6. The seven seas I shall control 7. My devotees will never be in want 8. Those who are in distress will find solace at My feet 9. If a man utters My name with love, I shall fulfil all his wishes 10. I shall not forsake My devotees in this life or the next 11. I shall always abide in the hearts of My devotees
The Dhuni and Udi — Sacred Fire and Ash
Central Ritual Teaching of Dwarkamai
Sai Baba kept a sacred fire (Dhuni) burning perpetually in Dwarkamai. The ash from this fire — Udi (उदी) — He used as His universal medicine. He would apply it to foreheads, give it mixed in water to the sick, and send it by post to distant devotees. The Udi represents His divine grace in material form. Many documented miraculous cures — from tuberculosis to plague to paralysis — are attributed to the Udi. The Dhuni still burns at Dwarkamai today.
Giving Dakshina — Ego Returned to God
Sai Baba's Method of Spiritual Transformation
Sai Baba would ask every visitor for dakshina (money). The amounts varied — sometimes ₹2, sometimes ₹500, sometimes the same person multiple times on the same day. All money was redistributed the same day to the poor and needy — Sai Baba never hoarded any. The purpose: each time a person gave consciously, their ego's grip on wealth weakened. Dakshina was not fundraising; it was surgical ego-surgery performed with infinite compassion.
Historical Context — British India Era
Sai Baba in the Nationalist Period (circa 1880–1918)
Sai Baba lived through the height of the Indian independence movement. While Bal Gangadhar Tilak and others were mobilising nationalist sentiment, Sai created a space of radical equality at Shirdi — Hindus, Muslims, Parsees, and Brahmin scholars sat together receiving the same prasad. Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Dadasaheb Khaparde (a prominent nationalist), and other independence figures were among His devotees. His mahanirvana in 1918 occurred one year before the Jallianwala Bagh massacre — marking the end of an era of peaceful spiritual synthesis.

The Cosmic Transmission — How Power Passes
In the Sripada Srivallabha Charitamrutam, Sripada explicitly states that He is Narasimha Saraswati (2nd avatar) and will be Swami Samarth (3rd avatar) — the same Dattatreya consciousness in successive bodies. The transmission is not mechanical succession but cosmic self-replication: the flame lights the next flame and both are identical. Each Sadguru's power is not diminished but amplified through the parampara.
Sripada's Prophecy of Sai Baba
In the Sripada Charitamrutam, Sripada Srivallabha states: "In the future, I will take the form of a divine being at a place called Shirdi. I will be known as Sai Baba. I am instructing Hanuman to incarnate and serve Me in that form as My devotee." This prophecy, made in the 14th century, is documented in the Charitamrutam as the cosmic pre-planning of Sai Baba's mission — 500 years before His birth.
What Connects All Four Avatars
All four Datta avatars share identical signatures: • Miraculous powers of healing, omniscience, and bilocation • Teaching through stories and parables rather than formal discourse • Radical equality — serving all castes, religions, and social classes • A central sacred seat (Pithapuram / Ganagapura / Akkalkot / Shirdi) • A perpetual sacred fire or lamp at their principal residence • Prophecy of the next avatar before departure • Continued presence and miracles after physical mahanirvana

🌿 Identical Healing Miracles Across Centuries

All three texts document nearly identical categories of miracles: restoration of the dead, cure of terminal illness (leprosy / tuberculosis / plague), restoration of sight, healing of paralysis, rescue from snake-bite, protection from epidemic. The Gurucharitra's revival of the Brahmin's wife is structurally identical to Sai Baba's revival of Lakshmibai's son. The same divine power, the same compassion, the same method — separated by centuries.
📿 Shraddha (Faith) as the Supreme Requirement
Book 1 — Sripada Book 2 — Narasimha Saraswati Book 3 — Sai Baba
In the Charitamrutam, Sripada states: "I do not examine birth or caste — I look only at the degree of faith." The Gurucharitra repeatedly shows that devotees who doubted received nothing while the most faithful received everything. Sai Baba named Shraddha as His first pillar and said: "Without faith, the Guru cannot work." The common thread across 600 years: faith is the only currency that purchases divine grace.
🕊️ Transcendence of Caste and Religion
Book 1 — Sripada Book 2 — Narasimha Saraswati Book 3 — Sai Baba
Sripada served food to untouchables at a time when this was unthinkable. Narasimha Saraswati initiated a cobbler (low caste) as a disciple and blessed him with liberation. Sai Baba welcomed Muslims, Parsees, and untouchables to sit alongside Brahmins in Dwarkamai. Each avatar, in their respective era, performed the identical act of radical equality — dissolving social hierarchy in the name of the Guru's universal grace.
📖 The Parayan (Complete Reading) Tradition
Book 2 — Gurucharitra Book 3 — Sai Satcharitra
Both the Gurucharitra and the Sai Satcharitra have established parayan (complete reading) traditions. The Gurucharitra's 7-day parayan is one of the most widely observed spiritual practices in Maharashtra. The Satcharitra's parayan (also 7 days) was explicitly instructed by Sai Baba Himself. Both texts promise that sincere parayan will cure illness, resolve crises, and grant liberation — making the books themselves living Sadgurus.

Shared Sacred Places — The Datta Parampara Network

These places appear in more than one of the three sacred texts — forming a living sacred geography that spans the Deccan plateau from coastal Andhra to central Maharashtra.

Ganagapura
Books 1 & 2 — Principal Datta Pilgrimage Centre
The seat of Narasimha Saraswati — the most important Dattatreya pilgrimage site. Located at the confluence of Bhima and Amaraja rivers, Karnataka. Sripada (Book 1) is said to have blessed this site in advance. Narasimha Saraswati (Book 2) made it His permanent residence. The Nirgunapad (footwear of Narasimha Saraswati) here is considered His living presence.
Akkalkot
Books 1, 2 & 3 — Swami Samarth's Seat
The seat of Swami Samarth — the bridge between Book 2 and Book 3. Sripada prophesied this site in the Charitamrutam. Narasimha Saraswati's departure "toward Kardali Vana" is linked to Swami Samarth's later emergence. Sai Baba explicitly acknowledged Swami Samarth as His predecessor. All three parampara texts mention Akkalkot as a node of living divine power.
Pandharpur
Books 1, 2 & 3 — The Lord of Maharashtra
The great Vaishnava centre on the Bhima river — home of Vitthala (Vittoba/Pandurang). Sripada (Book 1) is described visiting Pandharpur. Narasimha Saraswati (Book 2) blessed the Varkari pilgrims. Sai Baba (Book 3) was declared "Vitthala Himself" by Goulibuva — an elder from Pandharpur. The Datta parampara and the Varkari tradition of Pandharpur share the same geographic and spiritual network.
Kashi (Varanasi)
Books 1, 2 & 3 — City of Liberation
India's most ancient pilgrimage city appears in all three texts. Sripada (Book 1) is described visiting Kashi in His wanderings. Narasimha Saraswati (Book 2) spent critical years at Kashi performing tapas before His sannyasa. Sai Baba (Book 3) told devotees who wished to go to Kashi: "Your Kashi is here at Shirdi." The three texts collectively teach: where the living Sadguru is, all tirthas exist.
Kardali Vana
Books 1 & 2 — The Mystical Forest
The forest of plantain trees (kadali = plantain/banana tree) described as the permanent abode of Dattatreya and His predecessors. Both Sripada (Book 1) and Narasimha Saraswati (Book 2) departed for Kardali Vana — indicating they did not die but continued in an unseen realm. Swami Samarth claimed to come "from Kardali Vana." The forest is a symbol of the invisible dimension of the parampara that transcends physical death.
Pithapuram
Book 1 — Birthplace of the Parampara
The coastal Andhra town (East Godavari district) where Sripada Srivallabha was born and performed His early miracles. The Kukkuteswara temple here is one of the 18 Shakti Pithas of India. Sripada's presence transformed it into a Datta pilgrimage site. The Sripada Charitamrutam was narrated here, at the Sri Kukkuteswara Swamy temple. It is the fountainhead of the entire parampara described across all three books.
Kanipakam
Book 1 (Chapter 5) — The Svayambhu Vinayaka & Shankar Bhatt's Turning Point
📖 Holy Text: Sripada Srivallabha Charitamrutam (Book 1) — Chapter 5
📍 Location: Sri Varasiddhi Vinayaka Temple, Kanipakam village, Irala mandal, Chittoor district, Andhra Pradesh — one of the most ancient Ganesha shrines in South India
🔗 Chapter title: "Arrival of Shankar Bhatt at Tirupati — Visits Tirumala Das at Kanipakam"
🔗 Why pivotal: On his pilgrimage south, Shankar Bhatt halts at Kanipakam and meets Tirumala Das — a 70-year-old washerman-devotee who is himself a soul reborn through Sripada's grace (see Soul Transcendence tab). Tirumala Das gives Shankar Bhatt three essential directions: (1) begin writing the Charitamrutam; (2) seek Pynda Venkatappayya Sreshti and Vatsavayi Narasimha Varma in Pithapuram; (3) narrates the miraculous origin of the Kanipakam idol.
The origin of the Kanipakam idol: Tirumala Das recounts how three brothers, quarrelling bitterly over property, were divinely cursed. When they dug a well, they struck a rock from which their tools chipped away pieces — only to find them miraculously restored. Digging deeper, they found a svayambhu (self-manifested) Ganesha murti — the Varasiddhi Vinayaka — emerging from within the well-water. The brothers' wounds and inner strife were instantly healed the moment they beheld the form. This idol is considered to grow slightly in size each year and remains partially submerged in the spring water to this day.

Significance to the Datta Parampara: Kanipakam is the geographical and narrative hinge in the Charitamrutam — it is the meeting point between Shankar Bhatt (the pilgrim-author from Karnataka) and the South Andhra Datta devotional network. Without this stop, the Charitamrutam might never have been written, and the Vatsavayi connection — with its remarkable soul prophecies — would not have been recorded. The ancient Vinayaka of Kanipakam thus appears as the hidden orchestrator of the entire biography.

⏳ Time gap: Gurucharitra composed circa 1510 CE → Ramdas born 1608 CE → Dasbodh composed 1659 CE — ~98–149 years in the same tradition

He established 1,100 Hanuman temples across Maharashtra. His samadhi at Sajjangad (Satara district) remains a major pilgrimage site.
Sri Sridhar Swami Maharaj (Deglurkar)
1908–1973 CE · Named prophecy in Charitamrutam · Ashram at Varadapur, Karnataka
📖 Holy Text: Sripada Srivallabha Charitamrutam (Book 1) — explicit, verbatim, named prophecy
🔗 Exact words of Sripada: "Another elder brother of Mine will become famous as 'Sridhara'. My padukas will be installed in the place of My birth during the time of 'Sridhara' only." — Sripada identifies this soul as spiritually close to him ("elder brother") and ties the installation of His sacred padukas at His birthplace Pithapuram to the era of Sridhara's manifestation. This prophecy was confirmed in the English translation of the Charitamrutam by Malladi Govind Dixit.
⏳ Time gap: Prophecy circa 1340 CE → Sridhar Swami born 7 December 1908 (Datta Jayanti — the birthday of Lord Dattatreya himself) → ~568 years between prophecy and birth. Sripada's padukas were installed at Pithapuram during his lifetime, fulfilling the second part of the prophecy.
Who he was: Born as Sridhar Deglurkar in Lad Chincholi (Gulbarga, Karnataka). His mother Kamalabai had a dream in which Dattatreya himself indicated their next child would be an avatar — born on Datta Jayanti, the child was named Sridhar on the 12th day. His father died when he was 3; his only earning brother died when he was 10; he was an orphan at 11. Yet even at age 10 he consoled his grieving mother with a discourse on the indestructibility of the soul that left adults stunned. Sajjangad and Samarth Ramdas: Guided to Sajjangad (Samarth Ramdas's sacred samadhi) by his teacher Palnitkar Guruji, Sridhar served there for 3¼ years from Ashwin full moon 1927 — sweeping, cooking for 100 devotees every Thursday, carrying 50 buckets of water daily from Sontale lake, washing in its freezing waters thrice daily in winter wearing only a loincloth. After 1½ years, Samarth Ramdas appeared to him in a vision of blazing light — the first time in 300 years, the tradition records, that Ramdas had personally blessed a devotee. Ramdas gave him the title Bhagwan Shrimat Paramhansa Parivrajakacharya. Varadapur Ashram: In 1954 Sridhar Swami came to Varadahalli (Varadapur), Shimoga district, Karnataka — a hamlet in the Western Ghats — and established his ashram, erecting the Dharma Dhwaja as a call to revive Sanatan Vedic Hindu Dharma. He struck the earth with his right thumb and a stream of water sprang forth — the Shridhar Tirtha, which flows to this day. He left his body in 1973. His declaration: "Dutt ani mi vegla nahi" — Dutt and I are not two different forms. Considered an incarnation of Dattatreya by Swami Chinmayananda, Swami Sivananda, Gulvani Maharaj, Swami Swarupanand, Satya Sai Baba, the Sringeri Shankaracharya, and other saints of his era.

Gajanan Maharaj of Shegaon

🔗 Exact words of Sripada: "Samardha Ramdas himself will become famous as Gajanan Maharaj in 'Shiv Gaon' and become an avadhoota." — Sripada identifies Samarth Ramdas by name and prophesies his next incarnation as an avadhuta (a God-intoxicated saint beyond all social norms) in a place called Shiv Gaon — identified as Shegaon (Shegaon is in Vidarbha, historically called Shiv Gaon in some traditions).

Who he was: Gajanan Maharaj appeared at Shegaon (Buldhana district, Vidarbha, Maharashtra) in 1878 — no one knows his birth date, parentage, or early life. He was clearly an avadhuta — an enlightened one who has transcended all bodily awareness and social convention — echoing Sripada's prophecy precisely: avadhoota. Sant Dnyaneshwar (Jnaneshwar) Maharaj

⏳ Time gap: Dnyaneshwar's samadhi 1296 CE → Sripada Srivallabha born circa 1320 CE → Gurucharitra composed circa 1510 CE — Dnyaneshwar precedes the texts by 24–214 years, as their spiritual ancestor
Major works: Dnyaneshwari (Bhavartha Deepika) — greatest work of Marathi literature, a commentary on the Bhagavad Gita written at age 15–16; Amrutanubhav — a pure non-dual mystical text with no parallel in Indian vernacular literature. He attained Sanjeevan Samadhi at Alandi in 1296 CE at age 21.
Sant Namdev Maharaj

📖 Holy Text: Sripada Srivallabha Charitamrutam (Book 1) — same era, same geographical world
🔗 How mentioned: Not named in the texts, but Namdev is documented as a contemporary of Sripada (both active circa 1320–1350 CE). Time Dilation Across Planes of Existence

Modern parallel: Einstein's Special and General Relativity (1905–1916) established that time is not absolute — it passes more slowly under strong gravity or at high velocity. At extreme gravity (black hole boundary), time nearly stops. The ancient multi-temporal cosmos maps precisely onto relativistic spacetime. Gap between text and science: ~600 years for Charitamrutam; 500 years for Gurucharitra.
Non-local Consciousness — Bilocation & Entanglement
📖 All Three Texts · Modern parallel: Quantum Non-locality
What the texts say: Sripada, Narasimha Saraswati, and Sai Baba all demonstrate bilocation — appearing physically in two locations simultaneously, knowing events happening at a distance without communication, and acting on people miles away. What the text says: Sai Baba used Udi — ash from the perpetual wood-fire dhuni — as medicine for plague, cholera, tuberculosis, skin diseases, and fever. Modern parallel: Wood ash contains calcium carbonate, potassium hydroxide (a broad-spectrum antimicrobial), and activated charcoal. What the texts say: All three texts document an exact correlation: the depth of a devotee's faith determines the degree of their healing. Modern parallel: Psychoneuroimmunology (established 1975 by Robert Ader) has demonstrated that belief produces measurable changes in immune function, hormone levels, and neurological pathways. Placebo studies show genuine physiological healing — not just reported relief — from treatment the patient believes to be real. Nocebo (the reverse) produces genuine harm from inert treatment believed to be harmful. The ancient texts describe this effect 500–600 years before its scientific formalisation. Gap between text and science: 500–600 years.
